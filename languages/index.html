WHY NATIVE-LEVEL MATTERS

Translated marketing underperforms native marketing in every measurable metric

Bounce rates, conversion rates, search rankings, and engagement all suffer when content reads like it was written by someone outside the language. That is why native-level execution is priced into every scope from the first language.

Search engines rank native content higher than translated content

Google and other search engines evaluate content quality, topical depth, and user engagement. Translated content consistently underperforms native content on all three, because the phrasing, keyword patterns, and information structure do not match how native speakers actually search.

1

Buyers trust content that sounds like it was written for them

Awkward phrasing, wrong register, cultural mismatches, and unnatural keyword usage all signal that the brand does not truly operate in this language. Trust drops before the buyer even reads the offer. Native content avoids this entirely.

2

Multilingual SEO requires language-specific keyword research

Search demand does not translate. The terms buyers use in German, French, or Japanese are not word-for-word equivalents of English queries. Multilingual SEO starts with native keyword research for each language, built from that market's own search data.

3

Localization decisions belong in the strategy stage

Adapting marketing for a new language means rethinking value propositions, adjusting tone for cultural norms, and aligning with local buyer expectations. These decisions are made before any content is written, so every writer starts from the same brief.

DEFINITION

What multilingual digital marketing is

Multilingual digital marketing is the practice of planning, producing, and measuring marketing in every language your buyers actually use, across search, content, paid media, and social. What sets it apart from translation is the moment the language decision is made. In multilingual marketing it is made at the start, together with keyword research, offer framing, channel mix, and creative.

That changes what gets produced. A multi-lingual programme generates different keyword sets, different landing-page structures, different ad copy, and sometimes a different funnel shape per language, all sitting under one strategy, one measurement layer, and one contract. Because the measurement layer is shared, the results from every language add up to one comparable total.

In practice a multilingual digital marketing agency owns four things per language: search (native keyword research, hreflang, per-language site architecture), content production, paid campaigns, and social. Each of the four is planned against the same strategy and reported in the same format.

Onlinuum runs this model across 35 languages as productized scopes with a fixed price, defined deliverables, and a named senior expert accountable for each language. The operating model is human-led and AI-augmented. AI accelerates research and production, and native specialists own judgment, quality gates, and everything that ships. FAQ

Questions buyers ask about multilingual marketing

What is multilingual digital marketing?

Multilingual digital marketing is running search, content, paid media, and social natively in each of your buyers' languages. The strategy is shared, but keyword research, messaging, landing pages, and ad copy are rebuilt per language. Translation converts words after the strategy is written. Multilingual marketing makes the language decision before it, which is why the difference shows up in rankings, conversion rates, and trust.

What is the difference between multilingual and bilingual digital marketing?

Bilingual digital marketing usually means two languages handled by one person or one team, typically a home market plus one export market. Multilingual marketing means three or more languages run as a system, where the cost of adding the next language, the quality standard, and the reporting format are all defined in advance. The practical break point is around the third language: that is where one bilingual generalist stops scaling and you need per-language ownership with shared operating standards.

Do you use native speakers or translators?

Native speakers with marketing expertise. AI tools assist with research and first drafts, but every external-facing deliverable is created or reviewed by a native specialist before it ships. We do not deliver AI-translated content as a final product. Each language has a named senior owner with a visible profile, so you always know who wrote what.

How much does a multilingual marketing agency cost?

Every scope on this site has a fixed price that includes one language. Each additional language adds 60% of that base price, and each additional country adds 40%. Both modifiers are visible in the order cart before you contact anyone, so a three-language programme can be modelled up front. There is no per-word billing, no hourly retainer, and no separate onboarding fee per market.

Which languages do you cover?

Arabic, Bulgarian, Chinese, Croatian, Czech, Danish, Dutch, English, Estonian, Finnish, French, German, Greek, Hebrew, Hindi, Hungarian, Indonesian, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Latvian, Lithuanian, Malay, Polish, Portuguese, Romanian, Russian, Slovak, Slovenian, Spanish, Swedish, Thai, Turkish, Ukrainian, and Urdu. Each has its own page with the services and markets available in that language, plus the named experts where a specialist is already assigned. The operating model, pricing modifier, and quality gates are the same in every language.

Can you run one campaign across several languages?

Yes, and that is the normal case. One strategy and one measurement layer sit above the campaign; below it, each language gets its own keyword set, ad copy, landing pages, and negative keywords. Budget moves between languages based on numbers that are measured the same way in each one. ALVADI ran five campaign languages this way on a single Google Ads account structure.

How do you QA content in a language we don't speak?

Two layers. First, a second native specialist reviews every deliverable against a documented checklist covering terminology, register, factual accuracy, and search intent. The writer never signs off on their own work. Second, you get an English-language QA summary per deliverable explaining what was checked and what changed, plus a terminology glossary agreed with your team up front, so brand and product names stay consistent even where you cannot read the copy.

Can I start with one language and expand later?

Yes. Most multilingual engagements start with one or two priority languages, prove the approach, then scale to additional languages when the results support it. The operating model is designed for incremental expansion, and the language modifier means you know the cost of the next language before you commit to the first.

Do you handle multilingual SEO including hreflang?

Yes. Multilingual SEO is one of our core technical capabilities, including hreflang implementation, locale-specific URL structures, and language-specific keyword strategies that avoid cannibalization across your language versions.
