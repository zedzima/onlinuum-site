WHY A TRANSLATED AD STOPS BEING AN AD

A Good Ad Translated Word for Word Is a Bad Ad

The value of marketing copy sits in the effect its words produce on a specific audience, and that effect is exactly what substitution destroys.

A slogan is a compressed argument

Every campaign line compresses a reason to buy into a few words. Translate the words and you keep the compression but lose the reason: the local audience has different objections, different competitors and different reference points. Marketing transcreation rebuilds the argument first and only then writes the line.

1

Humour, rhyme and wordplay do not survive substitution

A pun exists in one language only. A rhythm that scans in English collapses in German, where the same sentence is forty percent longer. Transcreation in advertising plans for these as production constraints from the brief onwards.

2

Regulated claims change at the border

Superlatives, health claims, price comparisons and guarantees are policed differently in every market, and ad platforms add their own rules on top. Transcreation in marketing means the adapted line is checked against local advertising practice before it is handed over to you.

3

The brief is what crosses the border

What a writer in the target market actually needs is the intent: who this is aimed at, what it must make them feel, which words are untouchable and which are free. We ask for that brief together with the source file, and every route we deliver is written against it.

Machine Translation, Localization or Transcreation

All three move copy between languages. They make three different promises, and only one of them is about the effect on the reader.

HOW A TRANSCREATION PROJECT RUNS

From Approved Source Copy to a Line That Works Abroad

Four steps, in the order the pricing model assumes: foundation first, execution second, verdict last.

Brief

We take the approved source copy plus the intent behind it: the audience, the job the copy has to do, what must survive untouched and what may be rewritten.

  • Source copy, campaign intent and target markets
  • Untouchable claims, names and legal wording
  • Reference assets and competitors in each market

Foundation

For every new language we build the Language Style Guide & Glossary once: terminology, tone, forms of address and the adaptation rules later batches are measured against.

  • Glossary and do-not-translate list per language
  • Tone of voice and forms-of-address rules
  • Adaptation rules agreed before any copy is written

Re-create

In-market copywriters produce the copy, the slogan routes and the ad sets in the target language, with a rationale note wherever the wording deliberately departs from the source.

  • Copy, slogan routes and ad sets per language
  • Back-translation so non-speakers can approve
  • Rationale note for every deliberate rewrite

Validate

A second in-market reviewer runs the Cultural Adaptation Review: does the line work here, is the claim allowed here, would a local buyer read it as written by a local brand.

  • Independent in-market reviewer sign-off
  • Cultural and regulatory flags before launch
  • One revision round per language after your review

TRANSCREATION FAQ

Common Questions About Transcreation Services

Straight answers about scope, pricing and delivery.

How much does transcreation cost?

Cultural Adaptation Review starts at $400 for one market. Marketing Copy Transcreation starts at $900 for 5,000 words, Slogan & Campaign Adaptation at $600 for 3 concepts, Ad Copy Adaptation at $500 for 5 ad sets, and Language Style Guide & Glossary at $600 for one language. Every price is per target language.

Are transcreation services priced per word or per hour?

Per unit of work. Copy is priced by word volume, slogans by concept, ads by ad set, reviews by market and the foundation by language. A per-word rate is a poor fit for a slogan: three concepts can take a week and amount to thirty words, which is why the transcreation rate per word people search for only makes sense on the long-form row.

How are additional languages priced?

Each target language is quoted at the published tier for that language. There is no base language and no per-language surcharge on these task rows, because language is the unit of work here. The only thing that makes the second language cheaper than the first is that its foundation is bought once and reused by every later batch.

When do I need transcreation, and when is translation enough?

Use the one-second test. If a wrong word costs you accuracy, you need translation or localization. If a right word costs you the joke, you need transcreation. Documentation, specifications and legal text want accuracy. Slogans, campaign lines, ad hooks and landing-page headlines want effect.

Which content types need transcreation and which only need translation?

Transcreation earns its price on slogans, taglines, campaign concepts, ad sets, landing-page headlines and brand storytelling. Product specifications, help centres, policies, shipping information and technical documentation should be translated: they carry no rhetorical work to lose, and paying creative rates for them wastes budget.

What does a transcreation brief include?

The approved source copy, the audience and the job the copy has to do, the untouchable elements such as brand names, legal wording and mandated claims, the elements that may be rewritten freely, reference assets from the market, and the success criterion you will judge the result against.

How do you measure the quality of transcreated copy?

A second in-market reviewer runs the Cultural Adaptation Review and answers three questions: does the line work here, is the claim allowed here, and would a local buyer assume it was written by a local brand. Back-translations let a brand owner who does not speak the language follow the reasoning.

How long does transcreation take?

Language Style Guide & Glossary takes 5-7 business days per language and comes first. Cultural Adaptation Review is 3-5 days per market. Marketing Copy Transcreation is 7-10 days at the 5,000-word tier, and slogan routes are 5-7 days per language. Languages after the first run in parallel.

Is machine translation good enough for marketing copy?

It is good enough to understand a text and not good enough to sell with one. An engine optimises for a faithful sentence, while an ad is judged on whether a stranger stops scrolling. It also cannot tell that a claim is legal in one market and forbidden in the next. Use it for internal gist and buy transcreation for anything public-facing.

Can AI do transcreation, and do I need a native speaker?

AI accelerates the mechanical parts: term extraction, first drafts, variant generation, consistency checks against the glossary. The judgement stays human, and it has to come from a writer living in the target market. Being a native speaker gives you the grammar. Living in the market gives you the references, the competitors and the claims local buyers no longer believe.

Do you do certified, sworn or document translation?

No. We do not offer certified, sworn, notarised or legal document translation. If you searched for a translation agency near you to stamp a contract or a diploma, a local certified translator is the right supplier. This scope is remote, per-target-language marketing production: campaign copy, slogans, ad sets and the brand material around them.

Will the transcreated copy rank in that market?

Transcreation optimises for the effect on a reader, and search demand in a market is researched separately. Per-market keyword research is sold as International Keyword Research in our Cross-Market Setup scope, at $400 for 1 market, $1,000 for 3 markets and $1,500 for 5 markets. Run it first when the page has to be found as well as land.
