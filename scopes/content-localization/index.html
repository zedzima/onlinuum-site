A full post-edit of machine-translated copy to publishable quality, not a light readability pass. Suitable for high-volume, low-risk content where the source is clean and the target market is already understood.

WHY LOCALIZATION MATTERS

Localization Keeps the Meaning; Transcreation Keeps the Effect

A faithful translation can still fail in the target market, because it carries the source market's search logic and persuasion logic with it.

Translated copy inherits source-market search logic

The phrasing that ranks in English is rarely the phrasing buyers type in German or Polish. Word-for-word translation moves the sentence but keeps the English query behind it, so the page reads correctly and is found by nobody. Demand research per market is a separate job, sold on the Cross-Market Setup scope.

1

Translated copy inherits source-market persuasion logic

Proof points, risk language, and directness travel badly. A claim that reassures a US buyer can read as overselling in Germany and as vague in Poland. An in-market linguist adjusts register, evidence, and emphasis while the offer itself stays intact.

2

Machine output is a draft, not a deliverable

Modern engines produce fluent sentences and confident mistakes: inverted claims, invented product names, and terminology that drifts across a page set. That is why post-editing is priced here as its own task with its own evidence, and the edit-distance report shows what it fixed.

3

Where localization ends and transcreation begins

Localization keeps the meaning: the same message, produced natively for another market. Transcreation keeps the effect and lets the wording change completely, which is a different unit of work with a different price. That work lives on the Transcreation scope of work.

CONTENT LOCALIZATION FAQ

Common Questions About Translation and Localization

Straight answers about scope, pricing, and delivery.

How much does content localization cost per word?

We price by volume tier per target language. Content translation is $600 for 5,000 words (about $0.12 per word), $2,000 for 20,000, and $4,000 for 50,000; 100,000+ is custom-scoped. Machine translation post-editing starts at $400 for 10,000 words, and localization QA at $300 for 10,000 words.

What is the difference between translation and localization?

Translation moves the sentence into another language. Localization produces the same message natively for another market: terminology, register, claims, units, dates, and examples are adapted so the copy behaves the way the source does at home. Everything we sell here is localization; translation is the part of it that happens to be visible.

What is the difference between localization and transcreation?

Localization keeps the meaning and produces it natively in the target language. Transcreation keeps the effect and allows the wording, the image, and sometimes the argument to change completely. That is a different unit of work with a different price, and it is sold on the Transcreation scope of work.

What is localization QA?

An independent linguistic quality pass over copy that is already localized, reviewed in context on live or staged pages. You get a severity-classified issue log, a terminology and style-guide conformance report, and a sign-off record per language. It works on copy we produced and on copy you produced elsewhere.

When is machine translation post-editing the right buy?

When the volume is high, the source is clean, the market is already understood, and the content carries low commercial or regulatory risk: support articles, catalog descriptions, internal documentation. It is the wrong buy for money pages, claims copy, and anything a regulator reads. Every order includes a go/no-go note on that judgement.

Why is there no charge for a base language?

Because the language is the unit of work. There is no base-language line item and no percentage surcharge for additional languages: you pay for each target language you actually ship. The second language is genuinely cheaper than the first when its style guide and glossary already exist, since that foundation is paid once per language and then reused by every batch.

How long does localization take?

Content translation at the S tier (5,000 words, one target language) is delivered in 7-10 business days. M tier runs 2-3 weeks and L tier 3-5 weeks. Post-editing is faster at the same volume, and localization QA is typically 3-5 business days. Multiple languages run in parallel, so total turnaround does not multiply by language count.

Which languages do you cover?

The languages listed on our language capability pages are covered by named in-market linguists. Other languages are available through vetted partners under the same glossary, style guide, and QA standard. Tell us the pairs you need and we confirm coverage and turnaround before the order is placed.

What do you not offer?

We do not sell certified, sworn, or notarized translation, and we do not handle legal documents, patents, medical records, or interpreting. This is marketing and product content production. If you need a certified document, a specialist LSP is the right vendor, and we will tell you so before an order is placed.
