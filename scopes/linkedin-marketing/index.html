WHAT LINKEDIN MARKETING SOLVES

B2B Buyers Read People Before They Read Pages

On LinkedIn a company page builds credibility, while posts from founders and experts usually reach further and start more conversations. The work is running both with a plan.

A company page alone reaches few people

Company page posts mostly reach existing followers. Posts from the founder, executives and subject experts reach their networks and earn more replies, so the page and the people are planned as one calendar with different jobs.

1

Ghostwriting works when it sounds like the author

Every author starts with an interview about their expertise, opinions and the stories they can tell. Posts are drafted from those notes in the author's voice, and nothing goes live on their profile without their approval.

2

LinkedIn results are judged on pipeline

Impressions and likes say little in B2B. Reporting looks at who the audience is by job function and industry, profile views from target accounts, link clicks and leads, so the next month follows what moved the pipeline.

3
SUB-SCOPES

What Sits Inside LinkedIn Marketing

The work splits into three areas. Most B2B brands start with the company page and one author, then add people and advocacy.

LinkedIn company page management

The brand layer: page positioning, About section and showcase pages where they make sense, content pillars, the monthly calendar, post copy and visual briefs, publishing as page admin, and replies to comments on the page. The page is where prospects, candidates and partners check that the company is real and active.

A

Founder and executive ghostwriting

The personal layer: interviews, a topic plan for each author, drafted posts in their voice, and engagement support. The author approves every post and publishes it from their own account or through a scheduling tool they connect themselves. We do not log in to personal profiles or use automation tools that break LinkedIn's rules.

B

Employee advocacy

The network layer: a voluntary programme for employees who want to share company news and their own expertise. Guidelines cover what can be shared and how to disclose the connection to the company, and participation is tracked without pressure on anyone to post.

LINKEDIN MARKETING FAQ

Tasks, Ghostwriting, Base Module, and Languages

What do LinkedIn marketing services include?

Five tasks, bought individually or together: company page strategy and publishing, founder and executive ghostwriting, engagement and network management, employee advocacy setup, and reporting. In practice someone owns the calendar for the page and the authors, writes the posts, keeps the comment sections active, and turns last month's numbers into next month's topics. LinkedIn ads are a separate scope.

What is included in the $1,300 base module?

The standalone base module combines Company Page Strategy & Publishing S for eight posts ($500), Founder & Executive Ghostwriting S for one person and eight posts ($600), and Reporting S for the company page ($200). Engagement and employee advocacy are added separately.

How much does LinkedIn marketing cost?

The base module is $1,300 per month. Company page publishing scales from $500 for 8 posts to $1,300 for 24, and ghostwriting from $600 for one person and 8 posts to $2,400 for three people. Running every monthly task at the largest tier comes to $5,300 per month, plus a one-time employee advocacy setup from $400 to $1,400 depending on the number of employees. All prices cover one brand, one language and one primary country. You can configure the exact combination above and see the number before speaking to anyone.

How does LinkedIn ghostwriting work?

It starts with an interview about the author's expertise, opinions and stories, followed by a short call each month. A writer drafts the posts in the author's voice from those notes, the author edits or approves each one, and only approved posts are published. The author posts from their own account or through a scheduling tool they connect. We never ask for an author's LinkedIn password.

Do you comment and connect on behalf of the founder?

We reply to comments on the company page directly as page admin. For personal profiles we prepare the work and the author does the clicking: a list of accounts and conversations worth joining, drafted comments and replies, and connection priorities. LinkedIn's rules do not allow shared accounts or automation tools on personal profiles, and engagement pods and bought followers are not used.

Is this the same as a B2B social media agency?

For most B2B brands LinkedIn is the main network, so this scope covers the core of B2B social media marketing. If the brand also needs Instagram, Facebook, X or Telegram, those are planned in social media management, and the one-time audit and strategy scope decides which networks deserve effort at all.

Does this include LinkedIn ads?

No. Sponsored content, Lead Gen Forms, Thought Leader Ads and account-based campaigns are a separate paid scope with its own setup, management and ad budget. Organic posts that perform well are good candidates for Thought Leader Ads, so the two scopes work well together.

How are extra languages and countries priced?

Published prices cover one language and one primary country. Each additional language is added in the order, because posts for a second market need their own topics, examples and replies, written by a native speaker. Many B2B brands keep the company page in English and run local-language posts through authors based in each market.

Who owns the page, the profiles and the content?

You and your authors do. We work through named, revocable page admin roles, with an access log, and personal profiles always stay with their owners. Drafts, interview notes, calendars, guidelines and reports are yours, and the documentation goes with them if you move the work in-house or to another supplier.
