TEXT

Digital Marketing in Romania for International Companies

Romania is the largest of the European markets we opened pages for this month: 18,148,200 people, roughly $772bn of GDP and 97.7% literacy, inside the EU single market but outside the euro area. The size changes the arithmetic. Most small EU markets only make sense as one line inside a wider European plan; Romania is large enough to carry a programme of its own where the category has demand, and it is the Eastern European market most often budgeted alongside Poland in a regional rollout.

Language is where the real decision sits. Romanian is read by about 90% of the market, English by 31%, French by 12%, Spanish by 10% and Hungarian by 6.6%. The English share is high enough that a sales-led B2B or developer-facing product can start in English and still reach a serious audience, because Bucharest, Cluj-Napoca, Timișoara and Iași run a large technology and services sector that works in English daily. Anything consumer-facing, price-led or trust-sensitive needs copy written in Romanian from the start.

Romanian being a Romance language is the quiet practical detail on this page. If you already run Italian, Spanish or French markets, that copy is usually a cheaper starting point for adaptation than your English copy, because register, sentence rhythm and commercial vocabulary carry over more cleanly. That lowers the cost of a Romanian programme; it does not remove it. Romanian has its own vocabulary, a heavy Slavic loan layer and enough false friends that a mechanical adaptation reads wrong to a native speaker. The honest version is cheaper source material, same review gate. A line that has to be rebuilt from scratch falls under transcreation, which is priced as a separate scope from content localization.

We work as an outsourced, productized marketing service for companies based outside Romania that need the market covered without opening an office in it. Work that ships in Romanian is produced and reviewed by native Romanian linguists through our expert network. To be plain about staffing: there is no in-house Romanian specialist on the team today, the role is open, and until it is filled the accountable owner of your programme is a senior account lead who is not a native Romanian speaker. The open SEO expert role is where that hiring ask lives.

THE ROMANIAN MARKET

How the Romanian Market Actually Works

Four things that decide whether a Romania programme returns anything: where the demand sits, how Romanian is actually typed, how people pay, and where product discovery starts.

Bucharest first, then four regional hubs

Commercial demand concentrates in Bucharest, with Cluj-Napoca, Timișoara, Iași and Brașov behind it as real secondary markets. That is a wider spread than most small EU countries offer and it makes regional targeting worth doing. One regional note matters: about 6.6% of the country reads Hungarian, concentrated in Transylvania. For most categories that is not a separate programme. For regionally targeted retail, services and recruitment it is.

1

Romanian is searched without its diacritics

Romanian is written with ă, â, î, ș and ț, and a large share of commercial queries is typed without them. A keyword set built only from correctly diacriticised forms understates demand, and a site that only matches the diacriticised spelling underperforms its own rankings. Both spellings belong in the research, and the templates have to tolerate both. This is the single most common technical mistake we see on Romanian sites built abroad.

2

The leu, and how people actually pay

Romania is in the EU but not the euro area, so pricing, Merchant Center feeds and checkout run in lei. Card payment has grown quickly, but cash on delivery is still a normal option in Romanian ecommerce, which changes conversion tracking, return rates and the way an offer is framed. Budgeting a Romania programme in euro and shipping a euro-priced storefront is the fastest way to lose the price-sensitive half of the market.

3

Product discovery often starts on a marketplace

Google carries effectively all of Romanian search, so the search side is technically simple. What is not simple is that a large share of product discovery starts on eMAG, the dominant local marketplace. For consumer goods, an ecommerce plan that is only a search plan is incomplete here, and marketplace listings need the same Romanian quality bar as the site.

FAQ

Digital marketing in Romania: common questions

Do you work with international companies expanding into Romania?

Yes, that is our primary fit. Onlinuum is an outsourced, productized marketing service for brands based outside Romania that need the Romanian market covered. Bucharest agencies serve Romanian clients well; we serve international companies that want one partner and one operating standard across several European markets, with Romania as one of them.

Do we need to publish in Romanian, or is English enough?

It depends on the category. Romanian is read by about 90% of the market and English by 31%, and that English share is concentrated in the technology and services workforce of Bucharest, Cluj-Napoca, Timișoara and Iași. A sales-led B2B or developer-facing product can start in English and reach a real audience. Consumer-facing, price-led and trust-sensitive categories need Romanian, and need it written by a native Romanian speaker. We map that split at keyword and URL level before anything is written.

Does Romanian being a Romance language make localization cheaper?

Partly. Romanian sits structurally with Italian, Spanish and French, so if you already run one of those markets, that copy adapts into Romanian more cleanly than your English does: register, sentence rhythm and commercial vocabulary carry over. What does not carry over is vocabulary shaped by a heavy Slavic loan layer and a set of false friends that make mechanical adaptation obvious to a native reader. You save on the drafting pass. You still pay for the native review.

Do we need a local company or a Bucharest office to advertise in Romania?

No. Google Ads, Meta and the main local placements can be bought from outside Romania, and EU rules mean a company already selling into another member state has most of the compliance groundwork done. A local entity matters for invoicing, some regulated categories and certain offline placements; search and paid social run without one. One thing does need local setup: pricing and payment. Romania uses the leu, and cash on delivery is still a normal option in ecommerce.

Is Google the main search engine in Romania?

Yes. Google carries effectively all of Romanian search, so a Romania programme is a Google programme in practice and there is no local search engine to plan around. The complication is elsewhere: a large share of product discovery starts on eMAG, the dominant local marketplace, so for consumer goods the marketplace listing is planned as part of visibility from the start.

What does digital marketing in Romania cost, and how long does SEO take?

We quote fixed-price scopes, not local rate cards or hourly rates, so the price is the same whether the market is Romania or Germany and you can see it before committing. Timelines behave like any mid-sized EU market: technical and on-page work shows up within weeks, content and authority work needs two to three quarters before it moves revenue. English-language search demand for Romania as a market is thin, in the low hundreds of queries a month; demand in Romanian, inside your own category, is the number that decides whether the programme is worth running.
